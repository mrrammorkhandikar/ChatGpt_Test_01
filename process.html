Process

A clear path from idea to production.

Four steps, visible milestones, and room to adjust when the product teaches us something new. Select a stage to see what that conversation produces.

01

Stage 01

Explore & Plan

We learn the users, the current workflow and the outcome that would make the project worth it. Scope, priorities and a practical plan come out of that conversation.

What becomes clear

  • Who uses it, and which role does which job
  • The workflow as it exists today
  • The outcome that would make the work worth doing
  • Scope and priorities for the first build

Stage 02

Design & Prototype

Screens and flows are sketched before the heavy build. You can click through the product and correct the direction while changes are still cheap.

What you can react to

  • Flows for the repeated task
  • Screens with a readable hierarchy
  • A prototype you can click
  • Feedback before engineering goes deep

Stage 03

Develop & Test

We build in short cycles with regular updates. Features land with the checks that matter: roles, data, edge cases and the paths people actually use.

What gets checked

  • Features against the agreed scope
  • Roles and the data each role can touch
  • Edge cases, not only the happy path
  • The paths people actually use

Stage 04

Deploy & Support

Launch includes the environment, the handover and a plan for what happens next. Support stays available after the first release.

What launch includes

  • The environment the product runs in
  • A handover the team can follow
  • The release itself
  • Support after the first real users arrive

Communication

Updates you can follow without chasing them.

A shared sequence

Scope, the current cycle and what is next live in one thread. The project manager keeps that sequence visible.

Something to react to

A prototype, a working slice, or a release note. Conversation is anchored to the product, not a status slide.

Room to correct

When use shows a better path, the plan can move. The four stages are a spine, not a lock.
